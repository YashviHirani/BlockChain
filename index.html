The Problem

Ethereum is Powerful. But It's Expensive.

🐢

Slow

Ethereum mainnet processes about 15 transactions per second, so demand can quickly outrun available block space.

💸

Costly

During heavy activity, gas fees can spike above $50 per transaction, pricing out everyday users and smaller experiments.

🔒

Congested

Millions of wallets, bots, protocols, and apps compete for the same scarce block space every few seconds.

What Is Arbitrum

Arbitrum: Ethereum's Turbo Engine

Arbitrum is an Optimistic Rollup that runs transactions off Ethereum, bundles the results, and posts compressed data back to mainnet. It assumes transactions are valid unless challenged, which keeps fees low while Ethereum remains the judge. Users get a fast app experience without giving up Ethereum's security roots.

  • Batches thousands of transactions off-chain
  • Posts compressed proofs to Ethereum mainnet
  • Inherits Ethereum's full security guarantee

Web2 vs Web3

Ethereum vs Bitcoin

⛓️
🟠 Bitcoin

Digital Gold

  • Store of value
  • Proof-of-work consensus
  • Simple scripting
  • 21M hard cap
  • ~10 minute blocks
🟣 Ethereum

World Computer

  • Programmable blockchain
  • Smart contracts
  • EVM execution
  • DeFi and NFT platform
  • ~12 second blocks

Bitcoin is money. Ethereum is a platform.

Public Key vs Private Key

🔐
🔑 Public Key

Your Address

  • Safe to share
  • Like an email address
  • Derived from private key
  • Receives funds
  • Visible on-chain
🔐 Private Key

Your Secret

  • Never share it
  • Like your password
  • Signs transactions
  • Controls wallet funds
  • Losing it means losing access
Key→Signature→Verification

Public = your mailbox. Private = your house key.

Blockchain vs Traditional DB

🗄️
🗄️ Traditional DB

Private Records

  • Centralized server
  • Mutable records
  • Admin can edit or delete
  • Requires trust
  • SQL-based and fast
⛓️ Blockchain

Shared Ledger

  • Distributed ledger
  • Immutable history
  • No single owner
  • Trustless verification
  • Append-only and slower

A blockchain is a database that no one owns and everyone trusts.
